Strategic Assessment: Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)

## Executive Summary
This report evaluates the enterprise readiness of **Quantum-Resistant Cryptography (PQC)**, specifically for high-stakes industries like financial services [cite: 110]. With quantum computing predicted to break current encryption standards like RSA and ECC by 2030, this assessment focuses on the transition to NIST-standardized algorithms to ensure long-term data integrity and regulatory compliance [cite: 109, 110].

---

###  Critical Technical Findings

#### **1. Cryptographic Resilience**
* **Algorithm Standardization:** Lattice-based (FIPS 203), code-based (FIPS 204), and multivariate (FIPS 205) algorithms have reached standardization milestones but remain under continuous cryptanalysis [cite: 112, 176].
* **Hybrid Implementation:** Experts agree that hybrid models, which combine classical and PQC algorithms, are the most practical approach during the transition period [cite: 177].

#### **2. Operational & Integration Challenges**
* **Legacy Infrastructure:** Significant challenges exist in integrating PQC with existing Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), TLS libraries, and VPN endpoints [cite: 117, 178].
* **Crypto-Agility:** Many legacy systems currently lack the "crypto-agility" required to easily swap out cryptographic standards [cite: 178].
* **Performance Impact:** Trials by organizations like SoftBank show less than a 5% increase in latency for PQC-hardened communications, which is within tolerable limits for most enterprise environments [cite: 180].

#### **3. Economic & Compliance Analysis**
* **Migration Costs:** Estimated PQC migration for mid-sized organizations ranges from $300K to $750K [cite: 179].
* **Primary Expenses:** Major costs include software upgrades, specialized staff training, and hardware module replacements [cite: 179].
* **Regulatory Alignment:** Early adopters are expected to gain regulatory favor by aligning with NIST's PQC migration guidelines (NISTIR 8547) [cite: 181, 182].
